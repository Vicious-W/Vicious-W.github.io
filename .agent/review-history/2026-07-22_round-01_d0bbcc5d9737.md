## Executive summary

The commit builds successfully and establishes substantial groundwork: independently modeled reactor structures, a code-level control-rod-to-power response chain, heavier and stable glass physics, gesture-gated Web Audio, delayed loading, and a working fallback.

Three Major issues prevent acceptance:

1. The full-screen glass canvas blocks vertical touch scrolling, trapping touch-only users on the hero.
2. Replacing the old shader plane removed the required heavy-water layer without adding a Three.js water surface or volume.
3. The sound mapping omits the required collision impulse and rolling-contact inputs.

Reduced-motion mode also continues a nonessential WebGL/physics render loop.

## Major

### R-001: The full-screen glass canvas prevents vertical scrolling on touch devices

- Severity: Major
- Requirement: `PROJECT_SPEC.md` “主要用户流程” step 3 and “响应式” require users to scroll through all four sections on mobile; the first-screen interaction must remain usable with touch.
- Evidence: In a Playwright MCP context configured with `390 × 844`, `hasTouch: true`, and `isMobile: true`, the glass scene loaded with `touchAction: "none"`. A swipe from `(195,700)` to `(195,180)` left `scrollY` at `0` and the hero top at `0`. The canvas occupies the entire viewport. `style.css:131-139` sets `touch-action: none`, while `src/glassCubes.js:300-359` handles pointer gestures across that canvas.
- Location: `style.css:131-139`; `src/glassCubes.js:300-359`; first-screen `.glass-surface`
- Impact: Touch-only visitors cannot reach About, Selected work, or Contact by normal vertical swiping. This breaks a principal site flow on mobile.
- Reproduction:
  1. Open the built page in a touch-enabled `390 × 844` browser context.
  2. Wait until the hero has `glass-ready`.
  3. Swipe upward from the middle/lower part of the full-screen canvas.
  4. Observe that the glass interaction consumes the gesture and `window.scrollY` remains `0`.
- Expected: Users can vertically swipe past the hero while deliberate cube gestures still support grabbing and dragging.
- Actual: Every gesture beginning on the full-screen canvas is subject to `touch-action: none`, so vertical panning is disabled.
- Acceptance criteria: In a Playwright MCP touch context at `390 × 844`, a normal upward swipe beginning on the hero must advance `scrollY` and allow reaching `#about`, while a deliberate cube drag must remain operational.
- Suggested direction: Define gesture coordination that preserves vertical page panning, such as directional intent or a limited drag activation gesture, without prescribing a specific implementation.

### R-002: The final Three.js scene has no heavy-water surface or volume

- Severity: Major
- Requirement: `PROJECT_SPEC.md` “最终目标” item 2 and “第一屏” require a distinguishable middle heavy-water layer with optical or physical characteristics. The active task also lists water, Cherenkov glow, and their lighting relationship as separate required reactor systems.
- Evidence: The target diff removes the previous `ShaderMaterial` pool plane from `src/glassCubes.js` and replaces it with `createReactorModel()`. `src/reactorModel.js` creates the liner, floor, deck, reflector, grids, rods, pipes, two additive glow planes, and lighting, but no water surface/volume mesh, transmissive material, reflection/refraction, surface normal disturbance, or water update state. Normal Playwright renders at all three viewports show the glass directly over the core/deck and additive circular glow, without a separately readable water layer. Because the Three.js renderer is opaque, the native fallback canvas does not supply water optics after `glass-ready`.
- Location: `src/glassCubes.js:56-91`; `src/reactorModel.js:138-163`; `src/reactorModel.js:321-353`
- Impact: The required three-layer composition—reactor, heavy water, glass—is reduced to reactor/glow plus glass. It also regresses the water appearance previously supplied by the shader plane.
- Reproduction:
  1. Load the target build and wait for `glass-ready`.
  2. Inspect the hero at any required viewport.
  3. Trace the rendered scene through `createReactorModel()`.
  4. Observe that only additive glow planes occupy the pool opening; there is no water object or water optical behavior.
- Expected: A separately modeled or rendered heavy-water layer provides recognizable depth and water optics between the reactor and glass.
- Actual: The final scene contains reactor geometry and glow but no water surface or volume.
- Acceptance criteria: Add a distinct water system to the final Three.js scene with independently identifiable geometry/material/state and observable water optics or disturbance. Playwright MCP at all three viewports must show it separating the glass from the submerged reactor while retaining the native fallback.
- Suggested direction: Introduce a bounded water surface or volume compatible with the existing WebGL1/ANGLE and performance constraints.

### R-003: Physical sound mapping omits collision impulse and rolling state

- Severity: Major
- Requirement: `PROJECT_SPEC.md` “第一屏” requires collision impulse, relative speed, contact position, and motion state to determine impact/landing/sliding sound. The owner-approved active task additionally requires sliding and rolling sounds to be driven by persistent contact state.
- Evidence: `src/glassCubes.js:185-204` derives both `strength` and `velocity` solely from `contact.getImpactVelocityAlongNormal()` and passes only those values plus horizontal pan to `audio.impact()`. No solver impulse, contact multiplier, contact force, or equivalent impulse data is read. `src/glassCubes.js:361-394` removes the normal component and maps only maximum tangential relative speed to `setSlide()`. `src/glassAudio.js` exposes only impact and slide voices; there is no rolling-contact classification or rolling voice.
- Location: `src/glassCubes.js:184-204`; `src/glassCubes.js:361-394`; `src/glassAudio.js:85-145`
- Impact: Impacts with different physical impulses can produce the same strength when their normal speeds match, and rolling/rotational contact with little slip can remain silent. The current-stage physical sound model is therefore incomplete.
- Reproduction:
  1. Trace a cannon-es `collide` event from `onCollide()` to `audio.impact()`.
  2. Observe that only normal relative velocity and contact X position are used.
  3. Trace persistent contacts through `computeSlide()`.
  4. Observe that only tangential slip is measured and no rolling state is sent to the audio layer.
- Expected: Impact strength reflects both collision impulse and relative speed; persistent sliding and rolling states drive appropriate bounded sounds, with position and motion state included.
- Actual: Impact is velocity-only, and persistent audio covers sliding only.
- Acceptance criteria: The physics-to-audio mapping must incorporate actual solved impulse/contact-force evidence and distinguish rolling from sliding. Instrumented browser verification must demonstrate that these states produce correspondingly different output while stationary contacts remain silent and concurrency/peak limits remain enforced.
- Suggested direction: Collect post-solver contact impulse or force and classify persistent contacts using angular motion together with contact-point slip.

## Minor

### R-004: Reduced-motion mode continues the physics and rendering loop

- Severity: Minor
- Requirement: `PROJECT_SPEC.md` “动效、性能与可访问性” requires `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` to stop nonessential continuous animation while retaining a usable static image.
- Evidence: `src/reactorModel.js:383-385` suppresses reactor state changes, but `src/glassCubes.js:403-419` still executes `world.step()`, synchronizes every mesh, renders, and schedules another animation frame. `start()` at `src/glassCubes.js:421-438` does not check `reduceMotion`. Playwright MCP with reduced motion enabled continued receiving self-scheduled animation-frame callbacks after the scene had settled; the headless background context throttled these to five callbacks during a one-second sample.
- Location: `src/glassCubes.js:403-438`; `src/reactorModel.js:383-385`
- Impact: Users requesting reduced motion still incur continuous physics and GPU work for a visually static hero.
- Reproduction:
  1. Emulate `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
  2. Load the hero and wait for `glass-ready`.
  3. Count animation-frame callbacks after initial settling.
  4. Observe that callbacks continue and each executes physics plus rendering.
- Expected: The static reduced-motion frame remains visible without a self-perpetuating nonessential loop; interaction may render on demand.
- Actual: The normal loop continues indefinitely while the hero is visible.
- Acceptance criteria: After reduced-motion initialization and settling, a one-second idle sample must show no self-scheduled glass-scene animation frames, while the static scene and deliberate cube interaction remain usable.
- Suggested direction: Render once in reduced-motion mode and schedule additional updates only around explicit interaction or layout changes.
